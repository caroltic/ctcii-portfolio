# Changes made since Part II
> The most significant change I made since Part II was narrowing my target audience down to two groups: people who currently own dogs and people who want to get a dog in the future. In Part II, I was targeting one additional group:  international students who want to get a dog and eventually bring the dog back to their home country. However, after restructuring my narrative, I realized that this extra audience group no longer fit naturally into the overall story I was trying to tell. My project is meant to give a realistic overview of what dog ownership looks like, and about half of the project is based on my personal data and experience owning Casper, compared with vet suggestions and general guidelines. This perspective is most relevant to current and future dog owners, so I decided to focus fully on those two groups.

Because of this shift, I removed the section comparing my vet visits and fees in the U.S. versus China. Instead, I added a fourth section that shows a broader and more general comparison of cost and exercise differences across different dog breeds and sizes. I also added a longer call-to-action page that summarizes key insights from the project, while pointing out other important factors that may affect dog ownership.


## The audience

For Part III of this project, I decided to focus only on people who currently own dogs and people who want to get a dog in the future. After interviewing five people, at least two of them suggested that the international student section needed much deeper context to make sense. I realized that if I expanded the China vs. U.S. section to include exercise levels, routine changes, and environmental factors like weather, it would be very difficult to isolate the real cause of Casper’s different vet visit frequency. Too many variables were changing at once to draw a clear conclusion.

## Final design decisions

For most of my data visualizations, I used bar charts because they work best for comparing different groups. I also carefully decided on my color choices, where I am either using color to group categories or to emphasize key traits. The only place I used a line chart was in the section comparing Casper’s calorie intake vs. his daily calorie needs. I chose a line chart there because I wanted to show how his eating behavior shifts over time, and thus suggest the insight that a dog’s eating habits can be influenced by many different factors.


# Final thoughts

This project turned out very differently from what I first imagined when I saw the assignment, but I am really happy with how it turned out. It became a project that helped me reflect on my own life, while also being something that could genuinely help future and current dog owner. If I had more time, I would have liked to explore Shorthand more and add small animations, icons, and visual details to make the presentation feel even more lively. The part I was most excited about was writing the call-to-action page, because it gave me the chance to look back and think about what I wish I had known before I got Casper, and what I wish I had learned sooner to keep him healthier and happier. Overall, this project is very personal and meaningful to me. I truly enjoyed researching the data and working with Tableau, and I feel proud of how everything came together in the end.
